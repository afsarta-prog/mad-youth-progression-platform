01 — What this platform is

One place where every youth journey lives

Before 2025, MAD's aftercare data lived across 15 different chapter spreadsheets, WhatsApp messages, and informal updates. There was no shared count of active youth, no year-on-year trail of individual progress, and no way to see the full picture from one place.

The Youth Progression Platform is a unified dashboard that brings together the Youth Profile data and Youth Pulse survey responses for all active aftercare youth — across every chapter, every cohort, and every year the data exists.

For volunteers and chapter leads

Understand each youth you support

See their story, their stability, their SSG attendance, and what they need in the year ahead — in one profile, with both a human lens and an operational lens.

For the programme team

Plan with evidence, not guesswork

Budget forecasts, progression rates, at-risk youth flags, and year-on-year comparisons — all derived from the structured pulse cycle, not informal calls.

For leadership

See the programme at scale

How many youth progressed this year? Which chapters need attention? What is the projected budget? These questions now have a single, reliable answer.

For funders and CSR partners

Track impact over time, not just snapshots

A youth who joined MAD with no income and now earns ₹18,000 a month has a traceable journey. This platform is where that trail lives, year after year.

02 — The three tabs

Three views of the same data

The platform has three tabs. Each one is a different lens on the same underlying youth data. You do not need to use all three — go to the one that answers your question.

Impact

The organisation-level view

The Impact tab shows aggregate numbers across all 388 youth in the 2025–26 profile and 329 active youth in 2026–27. Each section is tagged with the pulse survey section it draws from — so you always know what question generated that number. Use this tab when you need to report on the programme, plan the next budget cycle, or understand the overall health of aftercare.

S0 — Cohort overview Who is in scope: age cohorts, chapter distribution, total counts.
S1 — Status breakdown Studying, working, both, looking — and which industries working youth are in.
S2 — Progression tracking 68% progressed this year. 21% delayed. 11% stalled. Movement, not just status.
S3 — Stability signals Wingman assigned? Regular mentoring? SSG rated effective? These explain why outcomes differ.
S4 — Financial stability Critical constraint, dependency, average and highest salary among working youth.
S6 — Forward planning Projected budget for 2026–27, transition-ready youth, expected exits.
Chapters

The chapter-by-chapter view

The Chapters tab shows all 15 active cities as cards. Each card shows the total youth count, active youth count, and a progress bar. Click any chapter to see the youth in that city. Click any youth to open their full profile. Use this tab when you are a chapter lead reviewing your own city, or when programme leadership wants to compare chapters.

Progress bar Shows what percentage of total youth in that chapter are currently active.
Youth tiles Each tile shows name, cohort, status, and MAD_CODE. Green = working, blue = studying.
Action flag A yellow "Action" label means the CO has noted something that needs follow-up.
Note on numbers The current dataset has sample youth. When connected to the live Google Sheet, all youth in that chapter will appear here.
Youth directory

The individual-level view

The Youth directory is a searchable list of all youth across all chapters. Search by name, city, or MAD code (format B-100XXX). Filter by status: Studying, Working, or Looking. Click any youth tile to open their full profile. Use this tab when you are looking for one specific youth or want to see all youth in a particular status or city.

Search Type any part of a name, city, or MAD code — results update as you type.
Status filters Studying / Working / Looking buttons filter the full list instantly.
MAD_CODE The unique identifier for each youth, displayed in green on each tile. Format: B-100XXX.
Opening a profile Click any tile to open the full profile below the tile grid. Click ✕ to close.

04 — The pulse survey sections

Every number traces back to a question

Every metric on the dashboard comes from the Youth Pulse and Progress Cycle — a structured survey filled by Wingmen after a phone call or in-person meeting with each youth. The survey has 7 sections. Each section appears as a tagged label (S0, S1, S2…) throughout the dashboard. Here is what each section captures and what decisions it enables.

S0
Youth identification
Name, MAD code, chapter, age, cohort (18–21, 22–25, 26–28).
→ Active youth count, base denominator for all reporting
S1
Current education and work
Whether studying, working, or transitioning. Course or job details, income range.
→ Life stage segmentation, differentiated support
S2
Progression since last year
Academic progress, employment changes, transitions, stagnation or setbacks.
→ Who is stuck or regressing — the transformation layer
S3
Personal stability and mentoring
Clarity on next year, mentoring consistency, SSG and Wingman effectiveness.
→ Quiet-risk youth, mentoring gaps, human capacity allocation
S4
Financial stability
Income, financial stress, dependency on MAD, family contribution.
→ Budget forecasting, taper or exit support decisions
S5
Living and family context
Living arrangement, environmental safety, family responsibilities affecting decisions.
→ Structural constraints that numbers alone cannot explain
S6
Looking ahead — next 12 months
Plans, expected status, course completion, scholarship and support needs.
→ Budget projection, exit planning, transition forecasting
S7
Address and document updates
Bank details, Aadhaar, ID updates, supporting documents.
→ Finance disbursements, compliance, operational accuracy
05 — Where the data comes from

From a conversation to a dashboard

The dashboard does not generate data. It displays data that exists because a Wingman sat with a youth — on a call or in person — and filled the pulse form. Here is how that conversation becomes a number on the screen.

🤝
Wingman meets youth
Phone call or in-person meeting. At least once a month.
📋
Pulse form filled
Sections S0 to S7. Wingman fills on behalf of youth.
📊
Google Sheet
Single source of truth. All sensitive data lives only here.
🖥️
This dashboard
Reads from the Sheet. Zero youth data stored on the platform itself.
✅
Decisions made
Budget, support, mentoring intensity, exit timing.

Privacy model

Bank details, Aadhaar, PAN, full address, and family crisis notes never leave the Google Sheet. Only non-sensitive fields (name, city, course, status, progression signals) appear on the dashboard. The dashboard shell itself stores nothing.

About the current numbers

The dashboard currently runs on a sample dataset of 33 youth. Once the master Google Sheet is connected, all 329 active youth will appear automatically. The structure, logic, and all calculations are already built — only the data source connection is pending.

The two data years

388 youth — this is the Youth Profile baseline from 2025–26. It is the structured snapshot taken during the annual profiling cycle: who is in the programme, where they are, what support they receive.

329 active youth — this is the live count from the 2026–27 pulse survey. These are the youth actively engaged right now. The difference between 388 and 329 reflects youth who have exited, transitioned, or are not yet re-enrolled for this cycle.

Every number on the Impact tab comes from the pulse survey. Here are the four numbers that matter most and what each one is actually telling you.

Research consistently shows mentoring consistency is the strongest predictor of outcome. A youth without a Wingman is at higher risk — the 38% gap is the most actionable number on the dashboard.

S6 · planning data
This projection comes directly from the S6 pulse responses — what youth say they will need next year. It is not a top-down estimate. It is an aggregated, documented ask from 329 youth.

On the 5-dimension progress bars in youth profiles

Each youth profile in the Central view shows five dimensions with a percentage and a year-on-year delta: Academic progress, Financial stability, Career clarity, Confidence, and Living stability. These are derived from multiple pulse survey sections — they are directional indicators, not exam scores. A 75% in Academic progress means things are broadly on track, not that the youth scored 75% in a test. The delta arrow (↑ +5%, ↓ -3%) is the most useful signal — it shows direction, not just current state.

07 — Glossary

Terms used in this platform

MAD Aftercare uses specific terminology that may not be familiar to everyone reading the dashboard. Here is what each term means.

Wingman A trained MAD volunteer paired one-to-one with a youth. The Wingman maintains regular contact, fills the pulse form, and is the primary human relationship in the youth's aftercare journey.
MAD_CODE The unique permanent identifier for each youth. Format: B-100XXX. This code links the youth across all data instruments — Youth Profile, Pulse survey, budget records, and the IWT tracker. Never changes even if the youth's chapter changes.
Aftercare cohort Youth are grouped by age: 18–21, 22–25, and 26–28. Different cohorts receive different support intensities. The Wingman allocation ratio is 1:2 for 18–21 youth and 1:3–4 for 22–25 youth.
Youth Pulse The annual structured survey filled by Wingmen on behalf of youth. It has 7 sections (S0–S7) covering identity, education, progression, stability, finance, living context, and forward planning. The data from this survey drives everything on the Impact tab.
YPC / Youth Progression Captain The chapter-level leader responsible for youth progression in a city. Previously called CO (Chapter Organiser) — that title no longer exists. YPC is the correct current term.
SSG / Self Supporting Group A monthly peer community session for youth. Attendance is tracked — the SSG dots on a youth profile (8 circles, filled or empty) show attendance across the last 8 sessions. A youth attending fewer than 5 of 8 sessions is a signal worth investigating.
Aftercare Circle The monthly planning meeting for Wingmen — held before the SSG meeting. It is where Wingmen plan, flag challenges, and coordinate. This is a volunteer-only space; youth do not attend.
Transition out When a youth exits the programme — either because they are fully self-sufficient, or because they have aged out. A youth who transitions out should have a Transition Document completed so their journey is not lost from the system.
Crisis levels (L-scale) MAD uses L0, L1, L3, and L5 for crisis severity. L2 and L4 do not exist in this framework. L0 = self-reliant. L1 = low intensity, handled by Wingman. L3 = moderate, involves the central team. L5 = severe, led by MAD Central.
Stability rating One of four values on the Impact tab: Stable and progressing, Stable but needs monitoring, Facing active challenges. This comes from S3 and S4 combined — it reflects both personal stability and financial situation.
Platform Commons The existing attendance and SSG tracking system used by MAD chapters. Data from Platform Commons feeds the SSG attendance dots visible on each youth profile.
08 — What to do with this

Your role in keeping this alive

A dashboard is only as good as the data feeding it. Here is what each person in the aftercare ecosystem needs to do — and when.

Wingmen

  • Fill the pulse form after every youth meeting
  • Open each youth profile before a meeting — know their story before the call
  • Use the Frontline view to check action flags
  • Flag anything significant in the CO notes field
  • Complete a Transition Document when exiting

YPCs and chapter leads

  • Review your chapter tab monthly — check for action flags
  • Ensure all Wingmen are mapped (check wingman field per youth)
  • Use Central view for programme reviews and budget conversations
  • Verify SSG attendance dots are accurate after each session
  • Flag any youth whose stability rating has worsened

Programme team and leadership

  • Use the Impact tab for all organisation-level reporting
  • Reference S6 data for next year budget planning
  • Cross-check progression rates against mentoring consistency
  • Use the dashboard for CSR and donor conversations
  • Prioritise connecting the live Google Sheet to make data real-time

The one thing that unlocks everything else

The dashboard currently runs on sample data. To make it live with all 329 active youth, the master Google Sheet needs to be published as a CSV and the dashboard URL updated with that link. This is a one-time, one-hour setup. Once done, every pulse form response will flow automatically into the dashboard without any manual update needed.
